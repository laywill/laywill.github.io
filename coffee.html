How I Brew

What started as wanting a decent cup at home has turned into a proper hobby, and I approach it the way I approach engineering. Dose, grind, water temperature and time all change the cup, and changing two at once tells you nothing about either.

Every recipe here started as a baseline and got better by making one controlled change at a time. Test-driven brewing, more or less.

They are not three routes to the same cup, either. The French press, the AeroPress and the Moka pot each make a different style of coffee with its own quirks, so each recipe is tuned on its own terms rather than against the other two.

Where I Learned the Why

James Hoffmann's How to Make the Best Coffee at Home was a birthday present, and it is the book I recommend to anyone who wants better coffee at home.

Recipes are easy to find. What the book gives you is the reasoning behind them: why grind size, ratio and water temperature matter, so a bad cup becomes something you can diagnose rather than something you guess at.

The Grinder

A burr grinder is the biggest single upgrade for coffee at home, and mine shows you do not have to spend much to get one. It is a Dualit CCG2, the best I could afford about ten years ago, and it is still going.

For what it cost, it has taken me unreasonably far into this hobby. The grind settings in the recipes below are for this grinder, so read them as a direction rather than a number to copy.

French Press

My daily driver, and well over 95% of what I brew. It is forgiving, easy to repeat, and scales to a whole working day.

I brew one cafetière in the morning and another in the afternoon. Half goes straight into my mug and half into an insulated flask as soon as it is plunged, so the second cup stays hot without sitting on the grounds and stewing. Two brews, four fresh cups.

The dose is deliberately lighter than most published recipes. Hoffmann's, at full strength, nearly blew my face off. I brought the dose down a step at a time until I had a good workday sipper, and it still holds its own against the high street specialty shops.

AeroPress

The one that travels: it lives at the office, and comes with me when I am away from home. It is compact, hard to break and quick to clean.

I brew it inverted. Grounds and water in, stirred several times over four minutes, then the filter on, flip it onto the cup and press with gentle pressure.

I do not rinse the paper first, which only adds stress. I do plunge until I hear the air come through, because it dries the puck out and makes it easier to knock out. Whatever the internet tells you, it does not change the cup: run an A/B and I promise you will not taste the difference.

Moka Pot

For espresso-style drinks at home, or when a recipe calls for something stronger than a French press can make.

The recipe is Hoffmann's, and most of it is about heat rather than dose. Start with a clean pot: a dirty one tastes burnt, not seasoned. Fill the base with boiling water so the coffee is not slowly cooked while the water comes up to temperature, assemble it quickly with a tea towel to save your fingers, and keep the hob as low as it will go, so the extraction is as slow as possible.

Grind medium-fine and level the basket rather than tamping it, so the water steams through the bed instead of channelling through a compacted puck. The moment you hear the first steamy sputter, take it off the heat and run the base under the cold tap to stop it there.
